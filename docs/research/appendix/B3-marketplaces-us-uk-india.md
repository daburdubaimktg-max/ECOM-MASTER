# Appendix B3 — E-commerce platform database: United States, United Kingdom and India

Research date: 2026-11-26. Scope: online platforms that matter for a mass-market hair, skin and oral-care brand owner. The structured records are in `data/research/marketplaces/US.json`, `GB.json` and `IN.json`; this appendix explains them.

## Summary

The priority formula puts the same kind of platform first in all three countries: the general marketplace or mass retailer with the largest audience and the best data access. In the UK and India the leading beauty specialist (Boots, Nykaa) comes next and would rank first if category relevance were the only test.

How to read the evidence:

- **No market share in this file is verified** by a regulator, an audited filing or an official statistic. The only official statistics are the US Census and UK ONS online-retail figures. Every platform share is a third-party or analyst estimate, or a company claim, and is labelled as such.
- **Traffic figures** are Semrush estimates of web visits from each country for October 2026. They exclude app use, which matters most in India and for grocery and delivery apps.
- **Brand presence** was checked on each platform's own public pages, published sitemaps or app-store listing and, for most UK retailers, on an independent price-comparison site. Where no route worked the record says UNVERIFIED; bot protection was not bypassed.
- **Scores** apply the brief's rubric: five criteria scored 1-5 with weights 0.20, 0.25, 0.20, 0.15 and 0.20. Platforms that could not be checked get a neutral 3 for brand coverage, or 2 where their positioning points to low coverage.

## 1. United States

### Landscape

US retail e-commerce was $347.3 billion in Q3 2026 (seasonally adjusted), 17.9% of total retail sales and 10.4% higher than a year earlier [1]. Two retailers dominate. EMARKETER forecasts Amazon at 39.7% of 2026 e-commerce sales and Walmart.com above 10% for the first time, with the top 18 retailers taking 75.1% [2]; this is a forecast read from a search-result summary, not a verified share. Similarweb's October 2026 ranking of shopping sites runs amazon.com, walmart.com, ebay.com, temu.com and etsy.com [3].

Semrush's October 2026 estimates show the gap in audience: amazon.com about 1.13 billion US visits [4], walmart.com 313 million [5], ebay.com 228 million [6], temu.com 174 million [7], target.com 121 million [8], cvs.com 89 million [9] and walgreens.com 65 million [10]. The beauty specialists are an order of magnitude smaller: ulta.com about 42 million [11] and sephora.com about 39 million [12].

Beauty is heavily digitised. NielsenIQ data reported by Retail Brew put online at 43% of the US beauty market, with Amazon gaining 2.1 points of share in a year; these are 2024 figures and are dated [13]. More recent NielsenIQ data rank TikTok Shop as the #4 US health and beauty retailer, with more than $4.4 billion of category sales in twelve months [14]. Estimates of Amazon's share of online beauty and personal care differ by source; one trade summary gives just under 47% for 2025 [15], which could not be checked against the original article.

### Why the top three

**Amazon** scores 5 on every criterion. It has the largest audience by a wide margin [4], its search results for 'dove shampoo' showed Dove alongside Pantene, Head & Shoulders, OGX, Suave and Native [16], and its Selling Partner API is public and documented, with a US marketplace identifier [17][18]. **Walmart** is second on overall weight: US e-commerce sales grew 23% and marketplace sales 49% in the quarter to 31 October 2026 [19], and its developer portal documents marketplace and supplier APIs [20]. Its score carries a caveat: walmart.com returned a robot check to every automated request [21], so its beauty assortment and brand coverage are UNVERIFIED. **Target** is third because its mass hair-care range was verified directly, with 1,000+ shampoo results that included Dove, Pantene, L'Oréal Paris, Garnier and Head & Shoulders [22], on top of about 121 million monthly US visits [8].

The drugstores follow closely. CVS has product pages for all thirteen checklist brands in its published sitemap [23] and Walgreens listed about 258 Dove items [24]. Either could replace Target in the first wave if the product owner values pharmacy-channel coverage over reach. Ulta's brand directory lists Garnier, L'Oréal Paris, Vaseline, Olay, Neutrogena, CeraVe and Cetaphil but not Dove, Pantene, Head & Shoulders, Colgate or Sensodyne [25].

### D2C landscape

No brand-owned site met the bar for a record. The largest beauty D2C sites sampled are small beside the retailers: Semrush estimates for October 2026 are about 2.7 million US visits for elfcosmetics.com [26], 2.1 million for glossier.com [27] and 1.8 million for theordinary.com [28], against about 42 million for ulta.com [11]. Mass-market brand sites send shoppers to retailers instead of selling direct; Sensodyne's US 'where to buy' page, for example, hands off through a retailer widget [29]. D2C was not researched beyond these traffic checks, so this section is indicative only.

### Quick commerce and delivery apps

Instacart handled $10.5 billion of gross transaction value and 91.4 million orders in Q3 2026 across more than 2,200 retail banners [30]. DoorDash reports that its US grocery and retail categories are growing at more than double the pace of restaurants, and it has agreed to buy Instacart for about $17 billion, with closing expected at the end of 2027 or in the first half of 2028 [31]. Retailers run their own same-day services: two-thirds of Target's digital business comes from same-day services [32]. Gopuff remains active as a private company and raised $250 million in November 2025 [33], but its web audience is small at about 2.5 million US visits [34]. Uber Eats draws about 17 million US visits [35] and was not researched further.

### Considered but excluded

- **iHerb**: a global health and wellness retailer with $2.9 billion of net sales and 15 million active customers in 2025 [36]. Only about 6.4 million of its 35 million monthly visits come from the US [37]. It does carry Dove [38], but it is supplements-led and its US audience is small.
- **Sally Beauty**: net sales of $3.73 billion in fiscal 2026, of which e-commerce was $441 million [39], and about 8 million US visits [40]. Its brand navigation shows professional lines such as ion, Wella and Clairol Professional, not the mass checklist brands [41].
- **Gopuff and Uber Eats**: small web reach or not researched; see the quick-commerce note above.
- **Not researched**: dollar stores, regional supermarket chains, Kohl's (which hosts Sephora shops) and Etsy. These are gaps, not judgements.

## 2. United Kingdom

### Landscape

Online sales were 29.3% of UK retail sales in October 2026 and peaked at 32.5% in November 2025 [42]. Semrush's October 2026 list of UK retail sites is led by amazon.co.uk with about 156 million UK visits, then ebay.co.uk (69 million), argos.co.uk (39 million), asda.com (26 million), boots.com (25 million), temu.com (23 million) and tesco.com (21 million) [43].

Grocery is concentrated. Worldpanel by Numerator puts Tesco at 27.8% of the grocery market, Sainsbury's at 15.5%, Asda at 11.3%, Morrisons at 8.3% and Ocado at a record 2.3% for the 12 weeks to 1 November 2026 [44][45]. Tesco reported £3.7 billion of UK online sales in its first half, an online market share of 36.7% excluding rapid delivery, and more than 1,000 sellers on Tesco Marketplace [46][47]. The share is Tesco's own figure.

For beauty and personal care, Mintel's 2026 report says Boots 'reinforces [its] market leading position' online, that supermarkets are the key destination for online purchases, and that 77% of adults bought the category online in the past year [48]. The share figures behind those statements are paywalled. Boots' accounts for the year to August 2025 show retail revenue of £5.26 billion, Boots.com revenue up 18.3% and about 17 million Advantage Card members [49]. TikTok Shop says it is the UK's fourth-largest beauty retailer after beauty sales rose 60% in 2025 [50]; a later report calling it the second-largest [51] could not be verified.

### Why the top three

**Amazon.co.uk** ranks first because its audience is more than twice that of any other UK retail site [52] and it is the only UK platform with a fully documented public API covering the UK marketplace [18]. **Boots** is second and is the stronger platform on category relevance: Mintel names it the online market leader [48] and it stocked seven of the nine mass products sampled [53][54][55]. It loses to Amazon on the formula only because boots.com blocks automated access and no API was found. **Tesco** is third as the largest online grocer, with £3.7 billion of UK online sales in six months [46] and six of the nine sampled products in stock [53][56].

The UK brand check used a different method from the other two countries. Boots, Superdrug, Tesco, Sainsbury's and Asda all refused automated access, so presence was read from Trolley.co.uk, an independent price-comparison site, for nine products [53][54][57][58][59][60][61][56][55]. Morrisons was the exception: its own search pages were readable [62].

### D2C landscape

No brand-owned site met the bar for a record. Sampled D2C sites draw modest UK traffic: about 1.0 million UK visits in October 2026 for charlottetilbury.com [63] and 0.4 million for theordinary.com [28], against about 25 million for boots.com [64]. Both are prestige or specialist brands; D2C for mass-market hair, skin and oral care was not researched further.

### Quick commerce and delivery apps

The dedicated rapid-delivery firms have gone. IGD reports that Getir and Gorillas exited in 2024 and that grocers and delivery platforms absorbed the demand; it forecasts growth of 10.7% a year to £5.5 billion by 2031, or 15% of online grocery, and puts Sainsbury's quick-commerce sales above £700 million in 2025 [65]. Tesco's own service, Whoosh, grew 37% in the first half and is on track for more than £500 million of sales this year [46], and Tesco groceries launched on Uber Eats in August and on Deliveroo in September 2026 [47]. Morrisons manages Uber Eats, Just Eat and Deliveroo orders through Instaleap [66]. Deliveroo has been owned by DoorDash since 2 October 2025 [67] and draws about 7.9 million UK web visits [68]; Uber Eats draws about 7.4 million [35]. Gopuff still receives some UK traffic, about 147,000 visits [34]. No information on Zapp was found.

### Considered but excluded

- **Cult Beauty** (THG): about 1.9 million UK visits [69]; its brand list is prestige-led, with CeraVe and La Roche-Posay the only mass or dermo brands matched [70].
- **Space NK**: owned by Ulta Beauty since July 2025 [71], about 4.0 million UK visits [72]; its brand A-Z is prestige only [73].
- **Savers**: about 1.4 million UK visits [74]; the site refused automated access, though Trolley.co.uk lists it as a stockist of Dove, Garnier and L'Oréal Elvive [53][58][56]. A candidate for a later wave.
- **Beauty Bay**: rescued in a pre-pack sale in March 2026 [75]; the site is live again [76] but draws only about 0.3 million UK visits [77].
- **Chemist Direct**: status unclear. Trustpilot states that the company's website has closed [78]; it merged with Pharmacy2U in 2016 [79] (dated). Treated as not operating.
- **Pharmacy2U**: calls itself the UK's largest online pharmacy [80] and draws about 1.9 million UK visits [81], but it is prescription-led and its shop page showed a maintenance notice on the research date.
- **Getir**: exited the UK in 2024 [65].
- **Not researched**: Argos, Waitrose, Co-op, Iceland, Poundland and Just Eat. Waitrose, Iceland and Poundland appeared as stockists in the Trolley sample.

## 3. India

### Landscape

Bain & Company estimates India's e-retail market at $65-66 billion of gross merchandise value in 2025, up 19-21%, with 290-300 million online shoppers, and expects $170-180 billion by 2030 [82]. There are no regulator-verified platform shares. Press-reported analyst estimates put Flipkart at 50-60% of e-commerce GMV with 220 million monthly active users [83]; a NewsBytes item, read only as a search-result summary, attributes the estimate to ICICI Securities and adds 25-30% for Amazon India and about 10% for Meesho [84].

Quick commerce is the distinctive feature. It carries 16-17% of e-retail GMV, about $10-11 billion in 2025, and has doubled annually since 2023 [82]. Datum Intelligence's August 2026 estimates of daily orders are 3.4-3.6 million for Blinkit, 2.4-2.6 million for Zepto, 1.3-1.45 million for Swiggy Instamart, 0.9-1.0 million for Flipkart Minutes, 0.6-0.7 million for Amazon Now and 0.5-0.6 million for BigBasket [85].

Redseer puts online beauty and personal care at about ₹52,000 crore in 2025, 2.4 times the 2022 figure, and says quick commerce's share of it rose from about 2% to about 16% in three years [86]. Motilal Oswal credits Nykaa with about 27% of online beauty and personal care, and adds that personal-care assortments are 'standardized and FMCG driven', so quick commerce 'could wrestle away some market share' [87]. That distinction matters for a mass-market portfolio: Nykaa leads in beauty, while shampoo, soap and toothpaste are replenishment purchases that suit marketplaces and quick commerce.

India is app-first, so web visits understate reach. Google Play install bands, which are cumulative, are 1B+ for Flipkart [88], 500M+ for Amazon India [89] and Meesho [90], and 100M+ for Nykaa [91], Blinkit [92] and Zepto [93].

### Why the top three

**Amazon.in** has the largest web audience in the set, about 267 million India visits in October 2026 [94]; its Beauty best-seller page showed L'Oréal, Garnier, Nivea, Vaseline and Parachute [95]; and the Selling Partner API lists an India marketplace [18]. **Flipkart** is a close second, with the largest estimated share of GMV [83], a documented seller API [96] and verified Dove and Colgate ranges [97][98]. **Nykaa** is third and is the category specialist: an estimated 27% of online beauty and personal care [87], quarterly GMV of ₹6,151 crore [99] and a claimed 2,400+ brands [91]. Its lower score comes from data access: nykaa.com refused every automated request [100] and no API was found.

Blinkit is fourth and should be treated as part of the first wave if the product can ingest vendor data. Its net order value was ₹20,940 crore in the September 2026 quarter, up 79% [101], and its app listing names Garnier, Nivea, Colgate and Lakmé [92]. BigBasket and JioMart score well on verified brand coverage because their published sitemaps list the checklist brands [102][103].

### D2C landscape

No brand-owned site met the bar for a record, but D2C matters more in India than in the other two markets. Business Today, citing Redseer, expects more than 150 new-age brands to pass ₹100 crore of revenue by 2031 [104]. The brand sites themselves are modest: Semrush estimates for October 2026 are about 3.2 million India visits for mamaearth.in [105], 3.0 million for beminimalist.co [106] and 1.2 million for plumgoodness.com [107]. These brands also sell through the platforms: Mamaearth, Minimalist and Plum all appear in Tira's brand sitemap [108] and in Nykaa's app listing [91]. Monitoring the platforms therefore captures most D2C competitor activity.

### Quick commerce

Six platforms handled about 9.5 million orders a day in August 2026, and the top three accounted for nearly four-fifths [85]. Blinkit had 2,751 stores at the end of September 2026 [101] and Instamart 1,240 dark stores in 133 cities with 14.8 million monthly transacting users [109]. The marketplaces are pushing in: Flipkart had more than 800 dark stores by April 2026 [110] and Amazon Now passed a $1 billion annualised run rate in September 2026 [111]. Zepto is private. A broker summary of its IPO filing shows 1,139 dark stores and about 48 million annual transacting users; it paused the IPO in July 2026 after receiving SEBI's observation letter in May [112]. Specialists have their own rapid services, such as Nykaa Now in 15 cities [113] and Apollo 24|7's 19-minute delivery in six cities [114]. Prices and availability vary by dark store and are shown only in-app, so data collection needs the platforms' brand reporting or a vendor; 1DigitalStack, for example, states that it tracks Blinkit and Instamart [115].

- **Tata CLiQ Palette**: active and sold through the Tata CLiQ sites [116]; a search on tatacliq.com returned 16 Dove items [117] and the site draws about 5.4 million India visits [118]. Too small for a record.
- **AJIO**: a fashion marketplace with about 18 million India visits [119]. Reliance launched AJIO Beauty in July 2026 with more than 1,500 brands [120]; the site refused automated access. Worth revisiting.
- **DMart Ready**: revenue of ₹4,094 crore in FY26 but operating in only 11 cities after leaving seven [121]; about 1.5 million India visits [122]. Its home page lists bath soaps, toothpaste and shampoos among popular categories [123].
- **Netmeds**: about 7.2 million India visits [124]; its home page lists Beauty and Wellness categories [125], but its search page did not return results to an automated browser and no further evidence was gathered.
- **FirstCry**: about 8.2 million India visits [126]; a baby-products specialist, not researched for adult personal care.
- **Flipkart Minutes, Amazon Now, Nykaa Now and Myntra's M-Now**: covered inside their parent platforms' records.
- **Not researched**: ONDC and regional grocery apps.

## Limitations and gaps

**Research constraints**

- The session's web-search allowance ran out part-way through the work. After that point, evidence came only from pages fetched directly, so some facts could not be cross-checked and some planned checks were not made.
- The following facts rest on search-result summaries because the underlying page could not be fetched: the EMARKETER share forecasts [2], the ICICI Securities attribution and the Amazon India and Meesho GMV shares [84], the GCI estimate of Amazon's beauty share [15], Superdrug's £1.7 billion sales figure [127], Costco's membership counts and digital sales in dollars [128], Ulta's marketplace details [129], Walgreens' store count [130], Boots' 17% online share [131], Asda's 2025 online sales decline [132], Nykaa Now's city count [113], Myntra's beauty mix [133] and the TikTok Shop UK 'second-largest' claim [51].
- Most facts in the 'considered but excluded' lists, including the iHerb, Sally Beauty, Gopuff, Space NK, Beauty Bay, Chemist Direct, Pharmacy2U, Tata CLiQ Palette and AJIO items, also come from search-result summaries. The same applies to the Sephora store counts and the Asda-Ocado announcement.
- Many retail sites refused automated access and were not pushed further: Walmart, Sam's Club pages, Kroger, CVS pages, Sephora (US and UK), Boots, Superdrug, Tesco, Sainsbury's, Asda, Savers, Nykaa, Myntra, Meesho pages, JioMart pages, BigBasket pages, Blinkit, Tira pages, AJIO, DoorDash and Gopuff. Their brand coverage is either UNVERIFIED or verified by another route, as each record states.
- The Kroger and eBay developer portals returned HTTP 403, so their API scope is UNVERIFIED. No account was created on any platform, so no API was tested and access terms were not confirmed.

**Data caveats**

- Semrush figures are modelled estimates for one month, cover web visits only and differ from Similarweb's. October precedes the peak season, and Semrush showed large month-on-month falls for several sites in October 2026, for example 34% for amazon.com [4], so the figures may understate typical traffic.
- Google Play install bands are cumulative and worldwide unless stated; they are not active users.
- Sitemap counts are counts of product URLs, not live availability or price. For Costco, Meesho, eBay and JioMart only a sample of sitemap files was read.
- The UK brand check covers nine products on one day through a third-party comparison site. Ocado did not appear for any of them, which may reflect the comparison site's coverage.
- Dated items (older than about 18 months) are flagged in the records: the NielsenIQ beauty figures for 2024, Purplle's FY25 revenue and the 2016 Chemist Direct merger. The Boots online-share figure and the online-pharmacy share estimate are about 17 months old.
- Vendor names such as Profitero+, DataWeave and 1DigitalStack are cited from the vendors' own websites [134][135][115]. Their coverage claims were not tested and are not a recommendation.

**Scoring caveats**

- Scores are judgements against stated anchors. Neutral placeholder scores were used where evidence was missing, most importantly for Walmart, Kroger, Ocado, TikTok Shop and Swiggy Instamart.
- The formula gives 40% of the weight to reach and data access, which favours general marketplaces. The category leaders Boots and Nykaa rank below Amazon for that reason.
- Ties were broken as the brief requires; where ties remained, further keys were added and are documented in each file's `ranking_method_note`.

**Not found**

- Amazon's UK net sales; Sephora's US or UK sales; CVS loyalty membership; DoorDash monthly users; UK shopper numbers for TikTok Shop; Sainsbury's 2026/27 online growth; current revenue for PharmEasy; Blinkit's monthly transacting customers; beauty-category sales for any platform other than TikTok Shop US and Nykaa.
- Whether Boots runs a third-party marketplace, and the status of Zapp in the UK.
